Parent FAQ

Good questions deserve plain answers.

Ages, pricing, schedule, safety, what kids learn, and how the Louisville program works.

Fall 2026

The next ages 8–11 journey begins September 28 and finishes with Demo Day on October 24.

What is Code the Future?

Code the Future is a hands-on, year-round AI literacy program and learning platform for kids ages 8–11 in Louisville, Kentucky. Kids learn what artificial intelligence really is by using it through game-like missions and in-person sessions at the public library.

What ages is it for?

The current live program is designed for kids ages 8 to 11. Young-teen and older-adult tracks are also forming; you can join those interest lists on the enrollment page.

How much does it cost?

Each module is $75, and the four-module journey is $225, a $75 savings. A $10 deposit holds a fall seat and is fully credited toward tuition.

When does the fall cohort meet?

The journey begins Monday, September 28, 2026. The hands-on Louisville sessions meet on Saturdays October 3, 10, and 17, followed by Demo Day on October 24.

Where does it take place?

Kids meet for hands-on sessions at the Louisville public library, then continue with game-like missions at home on the Code the Future platform.

Does my child need coding or AI experience?

No experience is needed. Missions start at the beginning and build step by step, so total beginners are right at home.

Is it safe, and what about my child’s privacy?

Kids join with a cohort code and no child email is required. Cohort chat passes through a profanity filter and is visible to staff, there are no ads, and the curriculum was shaped with a child psychologist. Read the full privacy policy.

Do kids use real AI?

Yes. Rather than watching videos about technology, kids use real AI tools in a safe, guided setting—to recognize drawings, test ideas, and build projects.

What will my child actually learn?

Kids learn what AI is and how it works through patterns, prediction, and training. They also practice asking good questions, spotting wrong answers, deciding when a human should stay in charge, and building with AI responsibly. See the curriculum.

What do we need at home?

A tablet or laptop with internet access. The platform runs in a web browser, so there is nothing to install.

Who teaches it?

Sessions are led by an industry operator. Code the Future was founded by Jon Nichols and Kenya Guarnieri, a child psychologist; both are Louisville parents.
