Big screen · stages 2–9 (slides!)📰 The two stories — talking points

🖼️ New format: each story is now a run of full-screen SLIDES — one picture, one line. Tap Next to pace them like a movie trailer; YOU are the narration. Four slides per story, the last one carries the discussion question.
Story 1 · The wow — 🔥 the AI that smelled smoke (4 slides)
  • Slide 1 (lone smoke wisp): let it breathe. "Fires start small. By the time a person sees it…"
  • Slide 2 (watchtower camera): real, this summer — states across the western US put AI cameras on mountaintops.
  • Slide 3 (firefighters): Arizona, real event — AI flagged a smudge, human analysts confirmed, crews stopped it at ~7 acres. Punch the word FIRE.
  • Slide 4 (the pattern): make the room say it with you — AI spotted it · humans decided · firefighters acted. Frame it as a superpower, not surveillance: an AI can look at a million trees at once, a brain can't — and no AI decides alone. The ask invites big dreams: "what BIG problem would you point that superpower at?" (Cures, oceans, space, lost dogs — all great answers.)
  • Sources if a parent asks: ABC News wire on western states' AI detection; WBUR On Point, "How AI is helping fight wildfires" (Jul 10, 2026).
Story 2 · The pause — ⚖️ the robot in the courtroom (4 slides)
  • Slide 1 (robot at the desk): real, this summer — lawyers in Australia, in a REAL murder trial, filed court papers an AI wrote for them. They didn't check.
  • Slide 2: the papers cited court cases that never existed — invented names, invented quotes. The kids will gasp; grown-up professionals skipped the check THEY do every day in the Studio.
  • Slide 3 (kid detective): the research kicker — studies find AI uses more confident language when it's wrong. "The wronger it is, the surer it sounds." Pause on that.
  • Slide 4: the rule — AI predicts. Humans verify. They've lived this all summer; the lawyers hadn't. Discussion ask lands on-screen.
  • Tone: funny-then-serious, zero fear. The kids come out feeling ahead of adults — because on this, they are.
  • Sources if a parent asks: AP/Yahoo coverage of the Australian murder-case AI filings (Aug 2025 case, wave of 2026 repeats — court-tracker logs show AI-error cases climbing); Axios, "AI is still getting things wrong, more confidently than ever" (May 30, 2026).

Keep this off the projector 😄📡 Trivia answer key — all 10, with the WHY

Q1 · What does a LOOP do?🔁 Repeats steps over and over

Why: loops repeat steps — how a game redraws 60×/second, how every app they demo today keeps running. Ask-back: "Where is the loop in the app YOU built?" (every one of them has an answer — plant this for the demos.)

Q2 · Exact steps for a computer are called…📋 An algorithm

Why: exact steps, followed exactly — mistakes included. Ask: "What happens if two steps swap places?" (It breaks. Order is everything.)

Q3 · Finding and fixing a mistake is…🐛 Debugging

Why: every kid in the room did real debugging this week. Name one live if the moment's right — Charlotte watched a single missing function take down half her studio yesterday. One missing piece, half the app dark. That's why builders test.

Q4 · What you type to tell an AI what you want…💬 A prompt

Why: clear thinking beats fast typing. Ask: "What's ONE word you added to a prompt this week that changed everything?"

Q5 · An AI does what you ___, not what you ___⌨️ ASK · MEANT

Why: the golden rule of the whole camp. Sloppy goal in, surprise out. Ties story 1 (humans decide) to story 2 (know what the machine is).

Q6 · v1, v2, v3… what's that called?🔂 Iteration

Why: build → play → notice → improve → ship. They lived it: five days, five versions. Ask: "Which version number are you demoing today?" (Devon's deep in double digits — let him flex.)

Q7 · Why test AI in a sandbox?🧱 Mistakes stay inside

Why: callback to session 3's escape story — the walls exist because powerful helpers get watched first, trusted second. Their own Studio preview runs in a sandbox too — that's why their apps can't touch the internet in the little window.

Q8 · Who sent the fire trucks?🧑‍🚒 Humans made the call

Why: straight from story 1 — attention check + the deepest lesson of the summer wearing a helmet. Ask: "In your app, what does the human decide that the AI never should?"

Q9 · Who built today's apps?🧒 The builders in this room

Why: the room answers in unison or you've done something wrong. 😄 The AI typed fast; every idea and every "no, make it MORE like this" was a kid's. Look at the parents when you read this one.

Q10 · What does it mean to SHIP?🚀 Release it — done beats perfect

Why: the perfect final question — the answer is what happens next. "In ten minutes, all four of you are officially shipped software builders." Straight into the champion board, prize, DEMOS.

The main event · 10:45🎬 Running the reveals

Mechanics
  • Open /platform/m4/demoday.html on the projector. Four cards, poster art, confetti on every ship.
  • Tap a kid → the three questions fill the screen while they answer out loud: What did I build? · Why did I build it? · What did I learn?
  • Then ▶ PLAY IT FULL SCREEN — opens their real app in its own tab (deluxe mode turns on automatically: full effects and sound). Kid drives. Esc / close tab to come back; their card now says ✓ SHIPPED IT.
  • Suggested order — momentum builds: Adam → Charlotte → Devon → Emery. Adam's arcade action opens hot; Charlotte's trophy is a guaranteed room-wide cheer (3 pipes!); Devon's is the epic; Emery's closer is the stunner — a real store on the real internet, and both features she requested on the platform board are live in it.
Coaching the three answers
  • Shy kid? Feed the first sentence: "Charlotte, finish this: I built a…" Then get out of the way.
  • For Q3 ("what did I learn"), accept anything true — "that I can" is a complete answer. Repeat it back louder.
  • If an app hiccups mid-demo, narrate it as the curriculum: "Ladies and gentlemen — a LIVE bug! What do real builders do?" Kids: find it, stay calm, fix one thing. Best possible ending, honestly.
  • If a kid shipped a newer version at the session, open their Studio full screen instead — the launcher note covers it.
